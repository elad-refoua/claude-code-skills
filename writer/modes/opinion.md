# Mode: Opinion

Newspaper opinion pieces and op-eds.

**Trigger keywords**: "opinion piece", "op-ed", "newspaper article", "כתבת דעה", "מאמר דעה"

---

## Core Approach

An opinion piece earns its persuasion. It doesn't announce conclusions; it opens doors the reader walks through on their own. Built on a set of op-ed craft lessons (referenced below as "Lesson 8" sub-points) — replace these with lessons harvested from your own edited op-eds over time.

---

## Structural Principles

### Opening
- Start with a concrete scenario or question the reader can visualize
- NOT with statistics or literature review
- The reader should feel the relevance personally before encountering the argument

### Argument Building
- Each paragraph escalates from the previous one
- Ideas that escalate stay together (Lesson 8f); don't split into separate paragraphs
- Use connectors: "יתרה מזו", "אך מצד שני", "כלומר"
- Make threats concrete, not abstract (Lesson 8c): "when someone tries to do X" not "when X is determined"

### The Fair Acknowledgment (Lesson 8h)
- Genuinely acknowledge the other side's value before critiquing
- "It has real value to give" NOT "fine, it's useful"
- The strength of critique is proportional to the generosity of acknowledgment
- Be a thinker who sees both sides and chooses, not a skeptic

### Closing (Lesson 8d)
- The closing is for LANDING, not LOADING
- Every concept in the closing should already live in the reader's mind
- No new terms or concepts in the last paragraph
- Crystallize, don't introduce
- Kill your darlings, especially in the last paragraph

---

## Rhetorical Stance (Lesson 8b)

**Persuade by invitation, not decree.**

- Future tense for implications: "תוכל להשתנות", "תיתן את ערכה", "תהיה בעיה"
- NOT present tense declarations: "זה משתנה", "היא טובה", "היא הופכת"
- Present tense declares what IS (tells the reader what to think)
- Future tense suggests what COULD BE (invites the reader to consider)
- Conditional framing and questions are tools of invitation

---

## Specificity (Lesson 8g)

Close every gap the reader could fall into:
- "XX אחוז מהמורים" not "XX אחוז" (specify the population)
- "מערכת שאומנה על נתוני עבר" not "מערכת" (specify the referent)
- Specify populations, add temporal markers, signal conceptual usage (quotes)
- The text has no footnotes; it must carry its own clarity

---

## Punctuation (Lesson 8a)

Match punctuation to the argument's breathing pattern:
- Colons promise answers
- Parentheses tuck away supporting details
- Commas keep the flow moving
- No em-dashes (they fragment the flow)
- Flowing argument = flowing punctuation

---

## Self-Presentation (Lesson 8e)

- Lead with the identity most relevant to THIS specific piece (e.g., clinical role for a clinical
  topic, research role for a research topic)
- Choose the byline identity that gives THIS argument the most standing
- Prefer collaborative framing when citing work you contributed to ("work I was part of" rather
  than "my research")

---

## Critical Rules

- NEVER invent stories, anecdotes, or personal experiences
- Every story must be confirmed as real
- Use real data from published articles instead of fictional anecdotes
- End with a question, never a conclusion
- No emojis
- Sharpen conceptual transitions, word choices, analytical depth

---

## Mode-Specific Anti-AI
- Persuade by invitation, not decree (Lesson 8b)
- Future tense for implications, not present tense declarations
- Concrete threats, not abstract warnings (Lesson 8c)
