# Mode: Presentation

Slide blueprints for academic and professional presentations.

**Trigger keywords**: "presentation", "slides", "deck", "מצגת"

---

## What This Mode Does

Creates a structured slide blueprint: the content architecture for a presentation. This is about WHAT goes on each slide and HOW the narrative flows, not about visual design.

---

## Blueprint Structure

For each slide, specify:

1. **Slide title** (short, active)
2. **Key message** (one sentence the audience should remember)
3. **Content elements** (bullet points, data, visuals, quotes)
4. **Speaker notes** (what to SAY, not what's on the slide)
5. **Transition** (how this connects to the next slide)

---

## Narrative Arc

Presentations follow the same storytelling principles as all writing:

1. **Hook** (1-2 slides): Problem or question that grabs attention
2. **Context** (2-3 slides): What we knew, what was missing
3. **Method** (1-2 slides): What we did (keep brief for general audiences)
4. **Findings** (3-5 slides): What we found, building in complexity
5. **So What** (1-2 slides): Why it matters, implications
6. **Close** (1 slide): The question to leave with (not a conclusion)

---

## Slide Principles

### Less Text, More Impact
- Maximum 4-5 bullet points per slide
- Each bullet: one idea, one line
- Key statistics get their own slide with large font
- Use builds (progressive reveal) for complex slides

### Data Visualization
- Tables and charts over raw numbers
- Highlight the finding, not just the data
- "31% of participants" → visual showing nearly 1 in 3

### Audience Calibration
- **Academic conference**: more methodological detail, citations on slides
- **General audience**: storytelling first, minimal jargon
- **Clinical**: practical implications, case vignettes if real
- **Policy**: numbers that matter for decisions

---

## Speaker Notes Guidelines

Speaker notes carry the narrative. The slides are visual anchors; the notes are the actual talk.

- Write in conversational first person
- Include transition phrases: "Now, this is where it gets interesting..."
- Note audience engagement points: "Pause here for audience reaction"
- Include backup data references (not on slide)

---

```markdown

```
