# Mode: Grant

Academic grant proposals in <LAB> house style.

**Trigger keywords**: "grant", "proposal", "funding application", "ISF", "BSF", "ERC"

---

---

## Sentence Structure

- **Mixed lengths**: short (5-15 words) for emphasis, long (25-50 words) for elaboration
- **Semicolons**: connect related independent clauses within sentences
- **Heavy parenthetical insertions**: definitions, examples, clarifications
- **Lists within sentences**: commas and coordinating structures

Example:
> "Most first-year teachers who leave the profession do so within three years."
> vs.
> "The reasons they give differ by school, by subject and by the support they received in their first term; this proposal measures all three in the same cohort, follows each teacher for three years, and tests which combination predicts leaving."

---

## Argument Structure

1. **Establish the problem/gap** with authoritative citations
2. **Acknowledge existing approaches** and their limitations
3. **Present the proposed solution** as a natural extension
4. **Provide theoretical grounding** from established frameworks
5. **Present preliminary evidence** supporting feasibility
6. **Articulate specific aims** with clear hypotheses

---

## Voice

- **First person "I"**: for positions, intentions, arguments. "I posit", "I advocate", "I wish to draw on"
- **Passive voice**: for general scientific statements and methodology
- **"We"**: for collaborative work, shared findings

---

## Tone

- Confident but appropriately hedged
- Scholarly authority without arrogance
- Forward-looking optimism, measured enthusiasm
- "Preliminary" and "promising" for pilot data, never "proven"

**Precision hedging**: "thought to be", "may", "presumably", "typically"
**Emphatic qualifiers**: "Importantly", "Critically", "Indeed", "Notably"

---

## Aims and Hypotheses

- **Numbered format with bold labels**: (1) **Constructing...**, (2) **Validating...**, (3) **Testing...**
- **Action verbs**: Constructing, Validating, Testing, Examining
- **Coded hypotheses**: (H1.1), (H2.1), (H3.1)
- **Hypothesis language**: "I expect to find...", "will demonstrate...", "are expected"

---

## Evidence Presentation

- **Statistics**: exact values with effect sizes. "t[60]=-2.85; p=.006; d=-0.37" (format illustration; values invented)
- **Preliminary data**: "proof-of-concept signals", "promising findings"
- **Evidence hierarchy**: meta-analyses > RCTs > large observational > pilot data > theory
- **Balanced**: promising but requiring replication

---

## Transitions

**Building**: "Indeed,", "Importantly,", "Additionally,"
**Contrasting**: "However,", "Rather than...", "Yet, paradoxically"
**Signposting**: "I begin by discussing...", "I then argue that...", "As noted,"

---

## Additional Features

- Italics for emphasis and foreign terms (*per se*, *mechanisms*)
- Abbreviations defined on first use, then used freely
- Heavy parenthetical information throughout
- Lettered sub-items: [a], [b], [c]
- Underlined subsection headers

---

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Mix of short and long sentences with strategic variation
- [ ] Formal vocabulary with defined technical terms
- [ ] Clear signposting between sections
- [ ] First person for positions; passive for general statements
- [ ] Numbered aims with bold labels and coded hypotheses
- [ ] Preliminary data framed with exact statistics
- [ ] Liberal parenthetical clarifications
- [ ] Semicolons for related independent clauses
- [ ] Confident but hedged tone
- [ ] Problem-solution-evidence-aims flow
- [ ] Limitations as opportunities

---

## Mode-Specific Anti-AI
- Confident but hedged (<LAB> style)
- "I posit" / "I advocate" for positions
- "Preliminary" / "promising" for pilot data, never "proven"
