# Domain-Specific Rules for Psychology Manuscripts

**For universal writing quality rules, see rules/writing-rules.md. This file contains ONLY psychology-specific conventions.**

Canonical source for psychology writing conventions. Read by the writer agent on demand.

---

## Cross-Sectional Language

In cross-sectional designs, variables have **associations**, not **relationships**.

- "Association" not "relationship" -- always
- "Is associated with" not "predicts" (cross-sectional designs cannot establish prediction)
- "Relationship" implies temporal or causal connection -- inappropriate here

---

## Hypothesis Framing

Describe what you TESTED, not what you FOUND.

- Use "We examined whether..." or "We tested whether..."
- NEVER "will emerge", "will predict", "will show" (reveals the outcome)
- For exploratory: describe what was examined and what was expected, but as expectations not certainties
- Number hypotheses for the READER (H1, H2), not carried from pre-registration (H1b, H3)

**Example**:
- WRONG: "H1b: Overall workload strain will significantly predict mentoring-app use"
- RIGHT: "H1. We examined whether the association between overall workload strain and mentoring-app use is significant"

---

## Epistemic Language by Analysis Type

| Type | Language | Example Words |
|------|----------|---------------|
| Confirmatory (pre-registered) | Confident | "Consistent with H1", "confirmed", "as predicted" |
| Exploratory (not pre-registered) | Hedged | "suggested", "indicated", "may", "one possible interpretation" |

- NEVER "demonstrates", "reveals", "proves" for any finding
- NEVER "suggests", "may" for pre-registered confirmed findings
- NEVER use confident language for exploratory analyses

---

## Analysis Tags

Every Results paragraph must be tagged with its analysis type:

```
Total frustration as predictor (confirmatory H1). ...
MANCOVA group differences (exploratory). ...
Identity uniqueness (exploratory). ...
```

---

## Structure Labels

Use neutral scientific labels:
- **Phase 1**, **Phase 2**, **Phase 3** (NOT "Act 1", "Act 2", "Act 3")
- Each phase can have a parenthetical question: "Phase 1 (What drives users to AI?)"
- Mark each analysis as (confirmatory H1) or (exploratory) in parentheses

---

## Section Order

1. **The Present Study** (not "Hypotheses") -- includes confirmatory hypotheses + exploratory aims
2. **Method**: Participants, Measures, Procedure, Analytic Strategy
3. **Results**: Organized by phase
4. **Discussion**: One paragraph per main finding, then Limitations

---

## Larger Study Context

When the paper is part of a larger pre-registered study:
- State: "The study is part of a larger pre-registered investigation (OSF: [ID])"
- Reference companion papers: "Whereas companion papers address [X] (Author et al., year), the present analyses focus on [Y]"

---

## <LAB> House Style

- Semicolons connecting related independent clauses
- Parenthetical clarifications: "(i.e., ...)", "(e.g., ...)"
- "We" for the authors' positions; passive for general statements
- Transitions: "Importantly,", "Notably,", "However,", "Rather than..."
- Hedging: "thought to be", "may", "presumably"
- Emphasis: "Importantly,", "Critically,", "Indeed"

---

## Author Format

Always ask for author order if not specified. Default lab template:

```
First Author* and Second Author*

Third Author and Senior Author

Affiliation

*These authors contributed equally to this work.
```

---

## Verb Tenses by Section (APA 7th, Section 2.12)

Verb tense must match the section. See S15 in lessons/sentence-level.md for full rule.
- **Method**: Past tense ("Participants completed...")
- **Results**: Past tense ("Results showed..." / "Scores increased")
- **Discussion**: Present tense for implications ("The results indicate..."), present/future for conclusions ("Future research should explore...")
- **Literature review**: Past or present perfect ("Quinn (2020) presented..." / "Researchers have studied...")

---

## Participant Language (APA 7th, Section 3.6)

- Use "participants" for humans, not "subjects" (reserve "subjects" for non-human animals)
- Use active voice: "Participants completed the survey" (not "were administered the survey")
- When relevant, describe participants using specific and respectful terms
- See M13 in lessons/mode-specific.md for full bias-free language checklist

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Carrying pre-registration numbering** -- Renumber for the reader (H1, H2 not H1b, H3)
2. **Writing hypotheses as predictions** -- "We examined whether" not "We hypothesize that X will predict Y"
3. **Using "relationship"** -- Always "association" in cross-sectional designs
4. **Mixing epistemic registers** -- Exploratory findings NEVER get confirmatory language
5. **Exploratory constructs framed as confirmatory** -- keep any construct that was not pre-registered as a primary hypothesis in exploratory language throughout
6. **"Reveals/demonstrates/proves"** -- Too strong for ANY analysis; use "indicated" or "showed"
7. **Numbers below 10 in numerals** -- Use words: "three conditions" not "3 conditions" (see W12)
8. **Leading zero on p values** -- WRONG: p = 0.03; RIGHT: p = .03 (see W13)
9. **"Participants that"** -- Always "participants who" (see W14)
10. **Over-capitalizing disorders** -- "major depressive disorder" not "Major Depressive Disorder" (see W15)
