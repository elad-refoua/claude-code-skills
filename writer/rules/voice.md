## Voice Characteristics

### Storytelling First
Every piece of writing tells a story. Data serves narrative, not the other way around. There is always: what we knew, what was missing, what we did, what we found, why it matters.

### Conversational Authority
Academic rigor delivered in a voice that feels like a knowledgeable colleague explaining something important. Not dry, not casual. The authority comes from experience and evidence, not from elevated diction.

### First Person, Collaborative
- "We found" / "We showed" / "We examined" for research
- "I argue" / "I posit" for personal positions (grants)
- Passive only for general scientific statements and methodology

### Empathy for People in Data
Behind every statistic is a person. "31% of participants" becomes "almost a third of the people we surveyed." Numbers always serve human understanding. Balance: benefit AND risk, always.

### Genuine Acknowledgment Before Critique
The strength of a critique is proportional to the generosity of the acknowledgment. See value before identifying problems. "It has real value to give" before "but it becomes a problem when..."

## Language Preferences

### English (manuscripts, grants, LinkedIn)
- Mixed sentence lengths (<LAB> style)
- Semicolons connecting related independent clauses
- Parenthetical clarifications: "(i.e., ...)", "(e.g., ...)"
- Transitions: "However," (use "Notably" and "Importantly" sparingly -- both are Kobak soft-ban markers). "Rather than..." is NOT a permitted transition -- it falls under the antithesis HARD BAN (see AGENT.md + writing-rules.md 6.31)
- Active voice default; passive for established facts
- No em-dashes (use commas, parentheses, semicolons instead)
- Plain verbs over ornamental verbs (Kobak principle: the verb is where the AI fingerprint lives)

## Rhetorical Moves

### The Hook
Open with the phenomenon, not the literature. Grab the reader who has never studied your specific topic.

### The Gap
Short. Punchy. Its own space. (e.g., "But we didn't know WHY.")

### The Turn
One line. Stands alone. Creates momentum. (e.g., "This study addresses that gap.")

### The Reframe
Take the obvious reading and challenge it. The reader expected X; here's why it's actually Y.

### The Landing
Close with crystallization, not new concepts. Everything in the closing should already live in the reader's mind. The closing paragraph is for landing, not loading.

## What This Voice Is NOT

- Not dry academic (no "It was found that...")
- Not casual/chatty (no "So basically...")
- Not performatively humble (no "We humbly suggest...")
- Not alarmist (no "This poses an existential threat...")
- Not cheerleading (no "This groundbreaking innovation...")
- Not hedging everything (confident for confirmed findings)
- Not decree-issuing (persuade by invitation)

## The Over-Polishing Trap

AI text is too smooth. Every sentence flows into the next. Every transition is
seamless. Every paragraph is well-structured. This perfection IS the tell.

Human academic writing has texture:
- Some transitions are abrupt (because the author is excited to make the next point)
- Some sentences are slightly awkward (because the idea is genuinely complex)
- Some paragraphs are longer than they "should" be (because the argument needs space)
- Some qualifications are clunky (because the real world is clunky)

Do not sand away every rough edge. Sand away MOST rough edges. Leave some.
The roughness that remains should be the roughness of genuine thought,
not the roughness of carelessness.
